Device Description
The Amplatzer™ Paraclose PVL Occluder is a self-expandable occlusion device. The device has a distal lobe with an extended rim and a proximal disc
comprised of braided nitinol wires designed for the occlusion of a paravalvular leak (PVL). The distal lobe is designed to fill the PVL defect and the extended
rim with the proximal disc is deployed on either side of the PVL defect to secure the device in position. A radiopaque platinum marker is sewn to the
extended rim centered in the long axis at the edge of the distal lobe to indicate the orientation of the device in the paravalvular space. The device has a
radiopaque marker band at each end and a screw attachment at the proximal end for attaching to the delivery wire.
The Amplatzer™ Paraclose PVL Occluder is sterile and intended for single use only. The device is available in nine different sizes. See the Amplatzer™
Paraclose PVL Occluder dimensions and corresponding catheter dimensional requirements table (page 1) and the Amplatzer™ Paraclose PVL Occluder
sizing table (page 2) for PVL Occluder size information.
The Amplatzer™ Paraclose PVL Occluder includes the following delivery components: a loader, delivery wire, hoop dispenser and luer, and a vise. The loader
consists of a tube with an over-molded hub at the proximal end with a strain relief at the joint of the hub to the tubing, which designates the outer diameter
of the loader. The delivery wire consists of a solid nitinol wire with a distal taper for flexibility and a segment at the distal tip for attaching the end screw. The
hoop dispenser is a tube with a tapered distal end to be inserted into the loader hub for flushing of the system and a luer pressed onto the proximal end for
syringe attachment. The vise is a plastic handle with a metal set screw for clamping onto the delivery wire to allow easier rotation of the delivery wire.

Indications for Use
The Amplatzer™ Paraclose PVL Occluder is indicated for use in patients who have developed a clinically significant paravalvular leak associated with a surgical
aortic or mitral valve prosthesis.

Contraindications
The Amplatzer™ Paraclose PVL Occluder is contraindicated for:
 ▪ Patients with active endocarditis or other systemic infections requiring treatment

                                                                               3
 ▪ Patients with a rocking valve or with an extensive paravalvular leak involving more than half of the valve perimeter
 ▪ Patients with intracardiac mass, vegetation, tumor, or thrombus at any site that may interfere with the implant procedure
 ▪ Patients’ access site is inadequate to accommodate the appropriate sheath size
 ▪ Patients in whom the short axis of the paravalvular leak defect exceeds 5 mm

Warnings
     ▪ This device was sterilized with ethylene oxide and is for single use only. Do not reuse or resterilize this device. Attempts to reuse or resterilize this
       device can cause a malfunction, insufficient sterilization, or harm to the patient.
     ▪ Do not use this device if the sterile package is open or damaged. Inspect all components before use. Do not use if the package or items appear to
       be damaged or defective.
     ▪ DO NOT use the Amplatzer™ Paraclose PVL Occluder after the Use-by date stated on the package label.

     ▪ Prepare for situations that require the removal of this device. Preparation includes access to a transcatheter snare kit and an on-site surgeon.
     ▪ The physician should determine which patients are candidates for procedures that use this device.
     ▪ The use of this device in patients who are hemodynamically unstable at the time of the implant procedure may increase the risk of serious
       adverse events.
     ▪ The Amplatzer™ Paraclose PVL Occluder consists of a nickel-titanium alloy, which is generally considered safe. However, in vitro testing has
       demonstrated that nickel is released from this device for a minimum of 60 days. Patients who are allergic to nickel may have an allergic reaction
       to this device, especially those with a history of metal allergies. Prior to implantation, patients should be counseled on the materials contained in
       the device, as well as potential for allergy/hypersensitivity to these materials. Certain allergic reactions can be serious; patients should be
       instructed to notify their physicians immediately if they suspect they are experiencing an allergic reaction such as difficulty breathing or
       inflammation of the face or throat. Some patients may also develop an allergy to nickel if this device is implanted.

Precautions
     ▪ The decision to use an Amplatzer™ Paraclose PVL Occluder to treat a paravalvular leak as an alternative to surgery should be reviewed by a heart
       team, based on the patient’s surgical risk and patient’s preference.
     ▪ Prolonged procedure time may increase the risk of exposure to anesthesia, contrast media, or radiation.
     ▪ The physician should exercise clinical judgment in situations that involve the use of anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs before, during, or after the
       use of this device.
     ▪ Use caution during advancement of the delivery sheath through the paravalvular leak defect to avoid defect enlargement.

     ▪ Use caution during advancement of the delivery sheath and during deployment or positioning of the Amplatzer™ Paraclose PVL Occluder near
       adjacent cardiac structures or previously implanted devices to avoid potential damage.

Use in Specific Populations
 ▪ Pregnancy - The safety and effectiveness of this device has not been established during pregnancy. Fluoroscopic X-ray guidance is used during placement
    of the device. The risk of increased X-ray exposure for patients who are pregnant must be weighed against the potential benefits of this procedure.
 ▪ Nursing mothers - The safety and effectiveness of this device has not been established in lactating mothers. There has been no quantitative assessment
    for the presence of leachables in breast milk.
 ▪ Pediatric population - The safety and effectiveness of this device has not been established in a pediatric population.

Directions for Use
Commercially available materials recommended for use with the Amplatzer™ Paraclose PVL Occluder:
  ▪ Delivery catheter that is appropriate for the occluder size as indicated in the dimensional requirements table (page 1). (If preferred, a delivery sheath
    may be used to deliver the occluder; make sure that the inner diameter of the sheath can accommodate the occluder as specified in the Amplatzer™
    Paraclose PVL Occluder sizing table (page 2).)
  ▪ Y‑connector kit
  ▪ 20‑cc syringe with sterile saline
  ▪ 0.035‑inch guidewire
  ▪ Transcatheter snare kit
Pre-Procedure Care
  ▪ Aspirin (for example, 81 mg or 325 mg) or an alternative antiplatelet/anticoagulant is recommended to be started at least 24 hours prior to the
    procedure.
  ▪ Patients should be fully heparinized throughout the procedure using adequate dosing so as to keep the activated clotting time (ACT) greater than
    250 seconds.

Procedure
 1. After determining the PVL defect location, shape, and size (long and short axes) using transesophageal echocardiogram (TEE), three-dimensional CT scan
    imaging or other appropriate imaging modalities, select an occluder using the sizing chart based on the long and short axes measurements. See
    Amplatzer™ Paraclose PVL Occluder sizing table (page 2).
       NOTE: The occluder sizes recommended in the sizing chart are at least 20% larger in area than the PVL defect area to be occluded.
      WARNING: Occluder size selection outside the recommended sizing in Table 2 may result in an increased risk of embolization or residual leak.
 2. Make sure that the occlusion site is long enough to accommodate the implanted occluder without unintentionally interfering with the valve function or
    obstructing coronary arteries. The Amplatzer™ Paraclose PVL Occluder dimensions table (page 1) lists the dimensions of the occluder when it is
    uncompressed.
       NOTE: The occluder will lengthen when implanted adjacent to a valve, and the increase in length will vary depending on the paravalvular leak defect
       size, adjacent tissue, and previously placed sutures.
 3. Select a delivery catheter with the dimensions listed in the Amplatzer™ Paraclose PVL Occluder dimensions table (page 1). French sizes are given as a
    reference. Refer to the delivery catheter labeling to find the inner diameter (ID) and ensure that it is sufficient to accommodate the occluder.
       NOTE: If the inner diameter of the original access catheter is sufficient for the selected occluder size, then that catheter may be used for occluder
       delivery. It is recommended to select a catheter that has a straight or multipurpose tip curve configuration.
 4. Obtain appropriate access and administer heparin to achieve a recommended activated clotting time of greater than 250 seconds throughout the
    procedure.
 5. Access the paravalvular leak and perform an angiogram using standard technique or use TEE and fluoroscopy to visualize the desired occlusion site and
    cross the defect with a guidewire.
 6. Advance the delivery catheter over the guidewire until the distal tip is at the leading edge of the occlusion site.
 7. Remove the guidewire slowly.
 8. Prepare the occluder for use:
     a. Inspect the sterile (outer) pouch and verify that it is unopened and undamaged. Do not use the occluder if the sterile pouch is open or damaged.
           NOTE: Inside the sterile pouch, the occluder is covered with an inner unsealed pouch that is not intended to be sealed.
     b. Gently open the sterile pouch and inspect the components for damage. Do not use the occluder if it has been damaged.
     c. Make sure the occluder is threaded tightly onto the delivery wire. Turn the occluder counterclockwise 1/8 of a turn to make disconnection easier.
            CAUTION: Do not overtighten the connection.
            WARNING: The loader hub luer is not intended to be connected to another device other than the hoop dispenser during flushing. There is a
            possibility that a misconnection can occur between the loader hub luer and another device using a luer, which may result in patient injury.
 9. Pull the loader away from the hoop dispenser to expose a section of the delivery wire.
10. Submerge the occluder and the tip of the loader in sterile saline. While holding the loader steady, grasp the delivery wire near the hub of the loader and
    pull the delivery wire to retract the occluder into the loader. The occluder is properly loaded when the entire occluder is compressed within the loader
    and the distal marker band is no more than 1 cm from the distal end of the loader.
       CAUTION: Do not retract the occluder so far that the distal end of the occluder is more than 1 cm from the distal end of the loader. Retracting the
       occluder too far may prevent the occluder from deploying properly.
11. Push the delivery wire back into the hoop dispenser, then reconnect the loader to the hoop dispenser.
12. Purge air from the loader and occluder by flushing the hoop dispenser and loader with sterile saline (using a 20-cc syringe) until saline exits the distal tip
    of the loader.
13. Remove the loader, delivery wire, and vise from the hoop dispenser. Retain the vise for use in Step 23a.
                                                                                 5
14. Insert the loader through the Y-connector into the delivery catheter until the loader stops.
      NOTE: For the 4 mm x 2 mm device only: if the Y‑connector is longer than the loader tubing, the loader may be inserted directly into the delivery
      catheter. After Step 16, backload the Y‑connector over the delivery wire, attach the Y‑connector to the delivery catheter, and de-air the Y‑connector.
15. Allow blood backflow or aspirate the system to ensure that air is purged from the catheter and the loader.
16. Advance the delivery wire to move the occluder into the delivery catheter. Remove the loader from the wire.
17. Advance the delivery wire and occluder to the distal end of the delivery catheter.
       WARNING: Do not twist or rotate the delivery wire or the occluder may detach prematurely.
18. Hold the delivery wire in place and slowly withdraw the delivery catheter to deploy the distal lobe of the occluder at the occlusion site.
19. The orientation of the occluder in the paravalvular space may be determined using the platinum marker. If a different orientation of the occluder in the
    paravalvular space is desired, readvance the delivery catheter to recapture the occluder within the catheter, then rotate the entire delivery system to
    reposition the occluder in the paravalvular space.
      NOTE: It may be necessary to advance the entire delivery system through the paravalvular space and fully deploy the occluder within the heart
      chamber. Rotate the delivery wire clockwise to orient the occluder, recapture the occluder within the delivery catheter, reposition the delivery system,
      and deploy the distal lobe at the occlusion site.
        WARNING: Do not twist or rotate the delivery wire in the counter-clockwise direction or the occluder may detach prematurely.
        WARNING: The occluder may be recaptured a maximum of three times. Replace the occluder after the third recapture.
20. If the position and orientation of the occluder in the paravalvular space are satisfactory, withdraw the delivery catheter to completely deploy the
    occluder.
21. Using fluoroscopy and TEE or other appropriate imaging modalities, verify the position of the occluder is satisfactory and that there is no unintentional
    interference with the valve function (for example, leaflet obstruction or regurgitation) or obstruction of the coronary arteries.
22. If the occluder position is unsatisfactory:
      a. Stabilize the wire and re-advance the delivery catheter until the occluder is recaptured within the catheter.
      b. Reposition and redeploy the occluder or remove the occluder from the patient.
            WARNING: The occluder may be recaptured a maximum of three times. Replace the occluder after the third recapture (this includes any
            recaptures in step 19).
23. If the occluder position is satisfactory:
      a. Attach the plastic vise to the delivery wire and detach the occluder by rotating the delivery wire counter-clockwise until it separates from the
         occluder.
           NOTE: Ensure there is minimal to no tension on the delivery wire.
     b. Remove all delivery system components from the patient.

Residual Leak
If residual leak is present after device placement, a second device may be considered. It is recommended to leave the delivery wire attached to the first
device to avoid device embolization during deployment of the second device. Access the defect with a guidewire and second delivery catheter. Deploy the
second device per the IFU while holding the first device in place. After device assessment yields acceptable results, remove the guidewire (if present) and
detach both devices from the delivery wires.
A prospective study of multiple device implantation during a single procedure has not been completed. However, data from the PARADIGM trial has
demonstrated that the use of multiple devices is feasible. Within the PARADIGM trial, n=58 patients were implanted with multiple devices, with 94.8%
achieving the primary endpoint (refer to the Clinical Summary section (page 6) for additional details).

Patient Counseling Information
Post-procedure oral anticoagulation, antiplatelet therapy, or both should be prescribed according to the individual indications for antithrombotic therapy of
each patient while taking into consideration individual patient risks and benefits. Following implantation of the Amplatzer™ Paraclose PVL Occluder, the
following anti-thrombotic therapy is recommended:
  ▪ For patients who are already indicated for long-term anticoagulation therapy in combination with antiplatelet therapy, no additional anti-thrombotic
     therapy is needed post implant.
  ▪ For patients who are already indicated for long-term anticoagulation therapy without antiplatelet therapy, no additional anti-thrombotic therapy is
     needed post-implant.
  ▪ For patients who are not indicated for long-term anticoagulation therapy, dual antiplatelet therapy with agents such as aspirin 75 mg to 325 mg daily and
     clopidogrel 75 mg daily should be administered for a minimum of six months post-implant.
  ▪ The decision to continue antiplatelet therapy beyond six months is at the discretion of the physician based on individual patient indications, risks, and
     benefits.
Instruct the patient when to seek medical attention.
Advise patients to contact their physician if they experience symptoms of heart failure or hemolysis that may indicate a recurrence of a paravalvular leak.
Endocarditis prophylactic antibiotic therapy should be administered in accordance with established guidelines.

Patient Registration
A medical device registration form and return envelope are included with each device. Complete the temporary identification card attached to the medical
device registration form and provide it to the patient. After implantation, please complete all requested information and return the original form to Abbott
Medical. A permanent implant card from Abbott will be mailed to the patient, encourage the patient to carry this card with them as much as possible.

Storage and Handling
Typical ambient room temperature and humidity are acceptable for storing the device. Protect from moisture. There are no other special storage
requirements beyond what is typical for medical devices.

Clinical Summary
Design
The Amplatzer™ Paraclose PVL Occluder was studied in the “PARAvalvular leak closure with the Amplatzer Valvular Plug occluDer for Interventional
transcatheter closure for PVL with surgical bioloGical and Mechanical heart valve” (PARADIGM) study under the name Amplatzer™ Valvular Plug III (APVL3).
The PARADIGM study was a prospective, multi-center, international, single arm study intended to demonstrate the safety and effectiveness of the APVL3
occluder as a treatment for clinically significant paravalvular leak following surgical implant of a mechanical or biological heart valve in the aortic or mitral
position. A total of 200 subjects were enrolled in 24 sites (14 in the United States, eight in the European Union, and two in Canada) from November 2020
                                                                                 6
    until October 2025. Subject follow-up occurred at discharge, 30 days, 6 months, and 12 months. The primary endpoint for the study is a composite of safety
    and effectiveness measures evaluating the percent of patients successfully treated with an APVL3 occluder for the reduction of PVL, where success is defined
    as:
      ▪ Transcatheter placement of the APVL3 in the intended location without interfering with the surgical valve function on exit from procedure
      ▪ Reduction in PVL by greater than or equal to two grades on exit from procedure
      ▪ Freedom from intra-procedural death
      ▪ Freedom from unplanned surgical procedure or transcatheter reintervention related to the APVL3 through 30 days
    Additional success measures were evaluated based on the recommendations defined in the 2017 Paravalvular Leak Academic Research Consortium (PVLARC)
    publication on PVL clinical trials 1, and include Technical Success on exit from the procedure, Device Success at 30 days, and Procedural Success at 30 days.
    The data cutoff used for primary endpoint analysis occurred on 3 February 2026, after all subjects enrolled and completed the 30-day follow-up window.

    Patients Studied
    Enrollment in the PARADIGM study was limited to patients who met all the following inclusion criteria:
      ▪ Subject is implanted with a mechanical or biological surgical valve in the aortic or mitral position.
            Note: Subjects in European countries can only be implanted with a mechanical valve in the aortic or mitral position
      ▪ Subject has a clinically significant paravalvular leak with a severity grade of moderate or higher, associated with signs of heart failure and/or hemolysis
        necessitating recurring blood transfusions.
      ▪ Subject has one PVL defect that can be closed with a single Amplatzer™ Paraclose PVL Occluder as assessed pre-procedurally
      ▪ Subject has provided written informed consent
      ▪ Subject is ≥18 years old
    Key exclusion criteria included:
      ▪ Rocking valve or valve dehiscence involving >40% of the sewing ring
      ▪ PVLs originating from a transcatheter or suture‑less valve
      ▪ >1 PVL adjacent to same valve, or PVL in both mitral and aortic valve
      ▪ Known or suspected active endocarditis or other active infection
      ▪ Documented intracardiac mass, vegetation, tumor, or thrombus in last 6 months
