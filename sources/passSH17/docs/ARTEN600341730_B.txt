Instructions for Use

Description
The Agilis™ NxT Steerable Introducer Dual-Reach™ set consists of a dilator
and steerable introducer, which is designed to provide flexible catheter
positioning in the cardiac anatomy. The inner diameter of the steerable
introducer is 13 F. The steerable introducer includes a hemostasis valve to
minimize blood loss during catheter introduction and/or exchange. It has a
sideport with three-way stopcock for air or blood aspiration, fluid infusion,
blood sampling, and pressure monitoring. The handle is equipped with a
rotating collar to deflect the tip clockwise ≥180° and counterclockwise ≥90°.
Actual curve angles will vary. The steerable introducer features distal vent
holes to facilitate aspiration and minimize cavitation and a radiopaque tip
marker to improve fluoroscopic visualization.

Indications for Use
The Agilis™ NxT Steerable Introducer Dual-Reach™ is indicated for the
introduction of various cardiovascular catheters into the heart, including the
left side of the heart, during the treatment of cardiac arrhythmias.

Intended Purpose
The Agilis™ NxT Steerable Introducer Dual-Reach™ is intended to introduce
various cardiovascular catheters into the heart, including the left side of the
heart through the interatrial septum.

Patient Target Group
The Agilis™ NxT Steerable Introducer Dual‑Reach™ is for patients requiring
placement of catheters in the cardiac anatomy, including transseptal access,
who have a vascular anatomy compatible with introducers that have an
outer diameter of 17.5 F.

Intended User
The Agilis™ NxT Steerable Introducer Dual-Reach™ is for use by clinicians
who are specially trained in percutaneous and transseptal access as well as
electrophysiology (EP) procedures.

Clinical Benefits
The intended clinical benefit of the Agilis™ NxT Steerable
Introducer Dual-Reach™ is to facilitate introduction of various cardiovascular
devices into the heart, including the left side of the heart through the
interatrial septum as measured by successful introduction of the device.

Contraindications
The Agilis™ NxT Steerable Introducer Dual-Reach™ is contraindicated for:
 ▪ Previous interatrial septal patch.
 ▪ Known or suspected atrial myxoma.
 ▪ Acute myocardial infarction.
 ▪ Unstable angina.
 ▪ Recent cerebral vascular accident (CVA).
 ▪ Patients who do not tolerate anticoagulation therapy.
 ▪ Patients with an active infection.
 ▪ Presence of an intracardiac thrombus.

Warnings
 ▪ Do not alter this device in any way.
 ▪ This device must be used by board-certified electrophysiologists, or EP
    fellows in training, in a fully-equipped operational EP laboratory.
 ▪ This device is intended for one time use only; do not reprocess or reuse
   it. Note the product “Use by” date on the package. Any attempt to re-
   sterilize and reuse this system may compromise its integrity. Adverse
   effects of using nonsterile components may include, but are not limited
   to:
   – Local or systemic infection or reaction
   – Mechanical damage
   – Inaccurate functionality
 ▪ Always aspirate, insert and withdraw components, and exchange
   catheters slowly to minimize the risk of air emboli.
 ▪ Aspirate all air before fluid infusion from the sideport.
 ▪ Provide continuous heparinized saline infusion while the introducer
   remains in the vessel.
 ▪ Fibrin may accumulate in or on the introducer tip during the procedure.
   To prevent dislodgement of potential thrombus, aspirate when removing
   dilator or catheter.
 ▪ Before removing the steerable introducer, reinsert the guidewire
   through the introducer, reintroduce the dilator over the guidewire,
   straighten the steerable introducer, then remove the dilator, guidewire,
   and introducer as a unit.
 ▪ Maximum in-vivo time: 7 hours.
 ▪ Read the IFU carefully before using this device to help reduce the
   potential risks and complications associated with the transseptal
   technique, such as air emboli and perforation of the aorta and left
   atrium.
 ▪ Aspirate and saline flush the introducer frequently to minimize the
   potential for thrombus formation.
 ▪ Do not use the introducer without a catheter or dilator supporting the
   lumen. Use of the introducer directly over a wire without a catheter or
   dilator supporting the lumen may result in complications that can cause
   death.
 ▪ For both patients and laboratory staff, cardiac catheterization
   procedures present the potential for significant x-ray exposure, which
   can result in acute radiation injury as well as increased risk for somatic
   and genetic effects due to the x-ray beam intensity and duration of the
   fluoroscopic imaging. Carefully consider the use of this introducer in
   pregnant women.
 ▪ Persons with known history of allergies to any of the materials listed
   below may suffer an allergic reaction to this device. Before use, counsel
   the patient on the materials contained in the device and discuss a
   thorough history of allergies. This device contains:
   – Polyether block amide (PEBAX)
   – Polytetrafluoroethylene
   – ABS
   – Silicone rubber
   – DOW Corning 360 fluid
   – HDPE
   – MDX/hexane solution
   – Nylon

Compatibility
 ▪ The French size specified represents the inner diameter of the
    introducer sheath.
 ▪ Do not attempt to insert a catheter having a distal tip or body size larger
    than the introducer size indicated.
 ▪ Do not attempt to use a guidewire larger than the maximum diameter
    specified on the package label.
 ▪ The Agilis™ NxT Steerable Introducer Dual-Reach™ is designed to
   interlock only with Abbott Medical dilators. Misuse may result in serious
   complications.
 ▪ Introducer materials are not compatible with magnetic resonance
   imaging (MRI).
 ▪ Only use a curved needle with a stylet, such as the BRK™ Transseptal
   Needle.
Preparation
 ▪ Before inserting the device into the patient, pre‑assemble the steerable
    introducer and the dilator.
 ▪ Before advancing the dilator or any other component, verify that the
   hemodynamics are acceptable. Monitor the hemodynamics throughout
   the procedure.
 ▪ Maintain an activated clotting time (ACT) of greater than 300 seconds at
   all times during use.
 ▪ If monitoring left atrial pressure during the procedure, take note of any
   negative pressure as it may increase risk of air ingress into the
   introducer.
Use with dilator/catheter
 ▪ Confirm the correct location of the needle on the fossa ovalis before
    advancing the needle.
 ▪ Avoid tilting the introducer handle upward during catheter or dilator
   insertion or withdrawal to avoid air ingress. Keep the handle at or below
   the level of the shaft. With the handle kept at or below the introducer
   shaft or heart level, blood or saline may leak from the introducer
   hemostasis valve during insertion. Leaking of blood or saline from the
   valve is a sign that the introducer is not filled with air, and is safe for
   catheter or dilator insertion.
 ▪ During insertion, do not create excessive bends in this device. This may
   inhibit advancement of the needle and may result in inadvertent needle
   puncture of the dilator/introducer assembly.
 ▪ During insertion, check for excessive resistance as the tip of the needle
   advances through the curvature of the introducer/dilator assembly. If
   there is any resistance to needle advancement, retract the needle, and
   inspect the components.
 ▪ During insertion, always use the stylet to facilitate needle passage
   through the dilator/introducer assembly. Failure to use the stylet may
   inhibit the advancement of the needle and result in inadvertent needle
   puncture of the dilator/introducer assembly or skiving of material from
   the inner surface of the dilator.
 ▪ If resistance is met when advancing or withdrawing the guidewire or
   introducer, determine the cause and correct it before continuing with
   the procedure.
 ▪ Aspirate the introducer slowly from the sideport to prevent drawing air
   across the hemostasis valve into the introducer.
 ▪ If aspiration is difficult, confirm that the introducer tip is not occluded or
   in contact with tissue.
 ▪ If aspiration during withdrawal is difficult, re‑confirm that the tip is not
   occluded or in contact with tissue.
 ▪ Do not torque or rotate the handle if the distal tip/shaft of the
   introducer is constrained.
 ▪ If the introducer shaft is unable to freely rotate, only manipulate the
   introducer via the articulation knob.
 ▪ Only inject or saline flush from the sideport.
 ▪ If pericardial or aortic entry occurs, do not advance the dilator over the
   needle. If the needle has penetrated the pericardium or aorta, it must
   be withdrawn. Monitor vital signs closely.
 ▪ Advance the introducer under fluoroscopic guidance to minimize the
   risk of cardiac damage, perforation, or tamponade. Compatible
   navigation and visualization systems may also be considered.
 ▪ Do not remove the dilator or the catheter rapidly. Damage to the
   hemostasis valve may occur.
 ▪ There is a risk of air ingress when inserting or withdrawing objects from
   the hemostasis valve of the introducer.
   To prevent vacuum buildup in the introducer, insert or withdraw objects
   slowly, ensuring the tip of the introducer does not become occluded.
   Rapid insertion or withdrawal may result in air ingress that could be
   visually observed as air bubbles in the sideport tubing or audible
   suction.
 ▪ After the introducer is inserted into the vasculature and the dilator is
   removed, and prior to any catheter insertion, aspirate until steady blood
   return is achieved before flushing or infusion.
 ▪ Immediately after every device insertion or removal, aspirate 20 to
   30 mL until steady blood return is achieved to mitigate air ingress.
 ▪ If the catheter is equipped with a straightener or valve bypass tool,
   avoid tilting the introducer handle upward while breaching the
   hemostasis valve as it may draw air in through the valve during catheter
   insertion. Remember to remove the insertion tool from the valve as
   soon as possible once the catheter tip has cleared the valve.
Post Procedure

    forth). Dispose of the device, its accessories, and packaging in

Suggested Transseptal Procedure
There are eight major steps in the transseptal technique:
 1. Prepare and assemble the equipment.
 2. Advance the introducer/dilator assembly into right atrium.
 3. Position the BRK™ Transseptal Needle inside assembly.
 4. Drag the assembly and engage fossa ovalis.
 5. Puncture the fossa ovalis with the BRK™ Transseptal Needle.
 6. Advance the introducer/dilator assembly over fixed needle.
 7. Advance the introducer over the fixed dilator and needle into the left
    atrium.
 8. Remove the dilator and needle from the introducer.

Prepare and Assemble Equipment
 1. Remove contents from packaging.
       NOTE: Do not assemble the introducer and dilator.
 2. Verify the deflection of the introducer prior to inserting into the patient.
 3. Flush the dilator and the transseptal introducer with heparinized saline.
       NOTE: Maintain the syringe on the dilator to prevent leaking
       NOTE: The fill volume of the introducer is approximately 15 mL.
 4. Close the introducer stopcock.
 5. Obtain/prepare a compatible BRK™ Transseptal Needle.
 6. Remove the stylet from the needle and flush the needle with
    heparinized saline.
 7. Insert the stylet into the needle and lock it onto the hub.
 8. Insert the dilator fully into the transseptal introducer.

Engage the Fossa Ovalis
 1. Visualize and identify anatomic landmarks.
 2. (Optional) If using fluoroscopy, set the unit to an appropriate angle
    parallel to the plane of the mitral valve and orthogonal to the plane of
    the septum. This will typically be approximately 30 to 40 degrees left
    anterior oblique (LAO).
 3. (Optional) The coronary sinus and His bundle catheter positions can
    serve as useful anatomic landmarks. In the appropriate LAO view, the
    coronary sinus catheter will be seen in profile. In the appropriate right
    anterior oblique (RAO) view the His bundle catheter will appear in
    profile. The fossa ovalis is located at or slightly below the level of His
    bundle catheter and superior and posterior to the coronary sinus
    ostium.
 4. (Optional) Placing a pigtail angiographic/hemodynamic monitoring
    catheter in the non-coronary cusp of the aortic valve can serve as a
    useful anatomic landmark.
 5. (Optional) Observe the pressure waveform being recorded through the
    BRK™ Transseptal Needle.
 6. Adjust the pointer flange so that the needle is perpendicular to the fossa
    ovalis (typically between 3:00 and 5:00 o’clock, as viewed from the foot
    end of the patient). (See the following figure.)

Advance Introducer/Dilator Assembly
 ▪ While maintaining a fixed needle position within the left atrium,
    advance the introducer/dilator assembly fully over the needle into the
    left atrial cavity.

Advance the Introducer Over the Fixed Dilator
and Needle into the Left Atrium
 1. Maintain the position of the needle and dilator across the septum.
 2. While maintaining the dilator in a fixed location, advance the introducer
    fully over the dilator into the left atrial cavity.

Withdraw the BRK™ Transseptal Needle and the
Dilator
  CAUTION: There is a risk of air ingress when inserting or withdrawing
  objects from the hemostasis valve of the introducer.
  To prevent vacuum buildup in the introducer, insert or withdraw objects
  slowly, ensuring the tip of the introducer does not become occluded.
  Rapid insertion or withdrawal may result in air ingress that could be
  visually observed as air bubbles in the sideport tubing or audible
  suction.
 1. Turn the needle stopcock to the off position and disconnect any
    attachments to the needle hub.
 2. Remove the BRK™ Transseptal Needle from the dilator. The needle may
    be cleaned and set aside for repeat use in this procedure. Otherwise,
    discard by appropriate means for contaminated sharp objects.
 3. Confirm that the introducer tip is not occluded or in contact with tissue.
    Immediately attach a 20 mL or larger syringe to the dilator and aspirate.
    Continue aspirating blood while holding the introducer in position and
    withdrawing the dilator. The blood should be arterial blood.
       CAUTION: If aspiration during withdrawal is difficult, re‑confirm that
       the tip is not occluded or in contact with tissue.
 4. Once the dilator is removed, aspirate blood through the sideport of the
    introducer, and then flush it with heparinized saline, taking care to avoid
    air bubbles.
 5. Provide 2 mL per minute minimum drip rate using a gravity IV
    administration bag. The gravity IV administration bag increases infusion
    if device removal creates a vacuum. This can prevent air ingress if the
    introducer tip is occluded.
 6. The introducer is now in place in the left atrium.

Catheter Introduction and Withdrawal
 1. Hold the proximal end of the introducer handle at or below level of the
    insertion site/heart level.(See the following figure.)
Figure 7. Handle Position




 1. Introducer handle position
 2. Insert device approximately 10 cm and slowly aspirate approximately 20
    to 30 mL from the introducer.
 3. Raise the proximal end of the introducer handle above the level of
    insertion site/heart level and slowly aspirate the introducer to prevent
    possibly air ingress through the hemostasis valve of the introducer prior
    to connecting continuous saline. Rapid aspiration may pull air in through
    the hemostasis valve.
 4. During catheter withdrawal, hold the proximal end of the introducer
    handle at or below level of the insertion site/heart level. (See the
    previous figure.)
 5. After withdrawal of any object through the hemostasis valve, including
    dilator, slowly aspirate approximately 20 to 30 mL from the introducer.

Remove the Introducer
 1. After the catheter is removed, prepare the introducer for removal by
    reintroducing the dilator, and ensuring neutral deflection.
 2. Withdraw the introducer.
 3. Dispose of the introducer in medical waste per site.
